# The Linchpin Framework

The operative frame for the Linchpin Designer pillar. Supersedes the classic Value Ladder.

**Key shift:** Value Ladder = product-sequence-first (free → low → mid → high). Linchpin = continuity-first. The recurring revenue offer is the anchor. Everything else exists to fill it.

---

## The four elements

### 1. Continuity (the Linchpin anchor)

The recurring revenue offer at the centre of the business. Membership, SaaS, subscription, retainer.

**If this doesn't exist, it's the gap. Build it first.**

Questions to diagnose:
- Does a continuity offer exist?
- What is the monthly/annual price?
- What does a member get that they can't get elsewhere?
- What is the churn rate? (If unknown, that's a problem too.)

### 2. MIFGE (Most Incredible Free Gift Ever)

The front-end entry point. So good that saying no feels dumb.

Two forms:
- **Free MIFGE:** lead magnet, free tool, free challenge, free video series. Recovers cost via email sequence → continuity upsell.
- **Self-Liquidating Offer (SLO):** low-ticket front-end (€7-€47) that recovers ad cost on day 1. The list is acquired free.

Diagnostic question: does the current front-end make people feel they got more than they paid for? If not, it's not a MIFGE.

### 3. Dramatic Demonstration #1 — Continuity sell

A one-to-many event (webinar, challenge, live training) that sells bulk continuity subscriptions.

**Default format:** Tiny Challenge — 5x 1-hour live calls, €0-€47 entry, sells continuity at the end. ~30% close.

**Alternative:** Perfect Webinar (90 min, one-to-many, stack slide close). Better for warm audiences.

### 4. Dramatic Demonstration #2 — High-ticket sell

A live or virtual event that sells high-ticket offers (€1,000-€10,000+).

Formats: live event, mastermind sell, intensive, done-for-you pitch.

## Linchpin diagnostic (3 questions — Linchpin Designer pillar)

Ask these one at a time. Use answers to identify the single missing/broken rung.

| # | Question | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do you have a continuity offer? If yes: what? If no: that's the gap. | Whether the anchor exists |
| 2 | What is your current front-end — how do new people find you and take a first step? | Whether the MIFGE exists and is working |
| 3 | What is your highest-ticket offer, and how do people get there? | Whether the Two Demonstrations exist |

---

## Revenue impact ranking (for "next rung" recommendation)

Rank missing rungs in this order when recommending what to build next:

1. **Continuity missing** → highest impact. No anchor = no compounding revenue. Build this first.
2. **MIFGE missing** → second. No front-end = no traffic leverage, no list growth. Build next.
3. **Demonstration #1 missing** → third. Continuity exists but no systematic way to fill it.
4. **Demonstration #2 missing** → fourth. High-ticket exists but no event to sell it one-to-many.

Never recommend building #2, #3, or #4 if #1 doesn't exist yet.

---

## Linchpin vs Value Ladder — when to mention the distinction

If the user says "value ladder," acknowledge the term, then reframe: "Brunson updated this to the Linchpin model. Same idea, but continuity sits at the centre rather than the top. Here's how that changes what you build next."

Do not lecture. One sentence of context, then move to diagnosis.
