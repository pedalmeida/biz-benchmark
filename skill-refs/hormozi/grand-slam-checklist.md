# Grand Slam Offer — Component Checklist

Use this when Mode 3 is mid-build or Mode 6 is mid-teardown. Every section is a filter — if the offer can't pass the test, name the hole and the fix.

The north star: **"Make it so good they feel stupid saying no."**

---

## 1. SCARCITY — quantity constraint (3 types)

Scarcity limits *how many* can get it. Mechanism: **fear of loss > desire for gain.** The ONLY rule that matters: **if you say "sold out," you must actually sell out.** Otherwise the tactic reverses and kills your credibility.

### The three types

1. **Limited Supply of Seats / Slots**
   - General or over a time window.
   - Example: "Only 40 seats. When they're gone, they're gone."

2. **Limited Supply of Bonuses**
   - The core offer stays available, but the bonuses run out.
   - Example: "First 10 buyers get the 1-on-1 onboarding call. After that, it's group onboarding only."

3. **Never Available Again**
   - Version/cohort/launch that explicitly will not reopen.
   - Example: "This is the last time I run this program. I'm moving into X next year."

### Service businesses — 3 cap tactics

1. **Total Business Cap** — "Only accepting 50 clients. Period." Periodically open +10-20% slots.
2. **Growth Rate Cap** — "Only 3 new clients per week."
3. **Cohort Cap** — "16 clients per class, 4 classes per year."

### Honest Scarcity (most ethical, most trusted)

State your real cap. "We are 81% to capacity." Implies social proof + scarcity. "Only you get to draw where 'full' is."

### Fail patterns (auto-reject in Grill mode)

- "Limited spots available" with no number → fake.
- Countdown timer that resets on refresh → fake.
- "Never again" used twice → fake forever.
- Scarcity announced but never sells out → the reverse signal.

## 2. URGENCY — time constraint (4 types)

Urgency limits *when*. "Deadlines drive decisions." **Last 3% of the time allotted creates 50–60% of the sales.**

### The four types

1. **Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency**
   - "Sign up today, kick off Monday — or wait for the next kickoff."
   - Works when fulfillment is batched.

2. **Rolling Seasonal Urgency** ← **Hormozi's #1 for local businesses**
   - Recurring date-capped promos with new wrappers.
   - "New Year Promo ends Jan 30" → "Valentines Lovers Promo ends Feb 14" → "Spring Reset ends Mar 31" → etc.
   - Always a fresh reason, always a close date.

3. **Pricing / Bonus-Based Urgency**
   - The promo, pricing, or bonus disappears — not the service.
   - "Price goes up Friday." "Bonus stack leaves at midnight."
   - Lets you run urgency year-round without closing the business.

4. **Exploding Opportunity**
   - The opportunity itself decays with time (arbitrage, early-mover ad-platform window, exploding job offer).
   - "Every second someone delays, they miss disproportionate gains."

### Fail patterns

- Urgency with no reason-why → weak.
- "Limited time offer" that has been "limited time" for 4 months → dead.
- Price goes up and doesn't → broken trust.

## 3. BONUSES — the splinter stack

**Core insight:** *"A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into its component parts and stacked as bonuses."*

Splinter into bonuses: **Products / Services / Next Logical Need.**

### The 11 Bonus Rules

1. **Always offer them.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Give each a special name with a benefit in the title.** ("No-Show Rescue Playbook" beats "appointment tool.")
3. **For each bonus, tell them:**
   - (a) how it relates to their specific issue,
   - (b) what it is,
   - (c) how you discovered or created it,
   - (d) how it improves their life (faster / easier / less effort — Value Equation still reigns).
4. **Provide proof** — a stat, a past client, a personal experience.
5. **Paint a vivid mental image** of life already using it.
6. **Always ascribe a price tag AND justify it.** ("Valued at €2,400, because…")
7. **Tools and checklists > additional trainings.** Lower effort to consume, higher perceived value.
8. **Each bonus addresses a specific concern or obstacle.** (Pull from Mode 1 productized objections.)
9. **Bonuses can solve the "next logical need"** — the problem the core offer creates.
10. **Total value of bonuses should eclipse the value of the core offer.** The bigger the stack, the bigger the implied core value.
11. **Enhance bonuses with their own scarcity + urgency.** ("First 10 buyers only." "This bonus leaves Friday.")

### Advanced: Other People's Products (OPM)

Negotiate with non-competing adjacent businesses to include their services as free bonuses in exchange for exposure. Then: negotiate a group discount for your customers + an affiliate commission for you. Your offer becomes profitable from bonus referrals alone.

*Pain Clinic example:* $400 core offer + stacked bonuses (massage, chiropractor, pharmacy discounts, PT session). Affiliate commissions on the bonuses = $350 profit per sale from bonuses alone.

### The anti-rule

**Never discount the core offer.** Add bonuses instead. Discounting is a position of weakness; bonuses are a position of strength. Discounting teaches buyers you are negotiable. Bonuses teach them you give more.

## 4. GUARANTEES — 4 types

**Thesis:** *"The single greatest objection for any product or service being sold is — drum roll — risk."*

Reversing risk is immediate conversion lift. Jason Fladlien: *quality of guarantee alone moves conversion 2-4x.*

**Universal template:** *"If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will Z."* **Never omit Z.**

### The four types

1. **Unconditional** — strongest pull for B2C / low-ticket. Highest refund risk, broadest take rate.
   - **"No Questions Asked" Refund** (Fladlien's framing: *"I'm not asking you to decide yes or no today — I'm asking you to make a fully informed decision."*)
   - **Satisfaction-Based Refund** (Hormozi's weight-loss guarantee — best-case / worst-case close.)

2. **Conditional** — "better than money back." Matches their investment with higher psychological commitment.
   - **Outsized Refund** — double or triple money back, or fixed-dollar penalty payment.
   - **Service Guarantee** — "we keep working for free until X."
   - **Modified Service** — capped extension (2x original duration).
   - **Credit-Based** — refund issued as credit toward any other service.
   - **Personal Service** — "I'll work with you 1-on-1 until X."
   - **Hotel + Airfare Perks** — reimburse ancillary costs.
   - **Wage-Payment** — pay the buyer's hourly rate if the call isn't valuable.
   - **Release of Service** — out of contract, free.
   - **Delayed Second Payment** — don't bill again until first outcome. ← **Hormozi's favorite.**
   - **First Outcome Coverage** — we pay ad spend until first sale.

3. **Anti-Guarantee** — "All sales are final."
   - Works when the product is copyable-once-seen. Requires a strong reason-why (exposure of proprietary process).
   - Signal: *"If you need a guarantee before taking the jump, you're not the type of customer we want."*

4. **Implied Guarantees** — performance-based. Skin in the game replaces refund risk.
   - Per-sale pay. Revshare. Profit share. Ratchets. Bonuses.
   - Pair with a minimum: "greater of $1,000 or 10% of revenue."

### Stacking guarantees (most powerful)

Combine unconditional 30-day + conditional 90-day triple-money-back. Or two conditional guarantees around sequential outcomes: "$10K by 60 days, $30K by 90 days if you follow the 3-step process."

### The math of guarantees

Close 130% as many people, refunds double (5% → 10%): 117/95 = **1.23x net.** The net is almost always positive — hence *"worth their weight in gold."*

### Name your guarantee

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Bad | "30 Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee" |
| Good | "30 days — if you wouldn't jump into shark-infested waters to get our product back, we'll refund every dollar." |
| Great | **"Club a Baby Seal Guarantee"** — "After 30 days, if you wouldn't club a baby seal to stay on as a customer, you don't have to pay a penny." |

### Warnings

- Guarantees attract bad customers. **Tie guarantee to consumption conditions** (they must complete X steps / attend Y sessions) to filter.
- **High-cost services:** use conditional or anti-guarantee. Unconditional + expensive fulfillment = double hit (refund + sunk delivery cost).
- **Start with service-based or performance guarantees** (no refund exposure). Move up the food chain as you gain conviction.

## 5. NAMING — M-A-G-I-C headline formula

**Principle:** *"Over time, offers fatigue. In local markets, they fatigue even faster."* Change the **wrapping paper** — same bundle, different wrapper.

**Use 3-5 of the 5 components. Not all. Shorter + punchier wins.**

### M-A-G-I-C

- **M**ake a Magnetic Reason Why (Attention)
  - Examples: Free, 88% Off, Giveaway, Spring, Grand Opening, New Management, Anniversary, Halloween, New Year.

- **A**nnounce the Avatar (Discrimination)
  - Examples: Bee Cave Dentists, Rolling Hills Moms, Salon Owners, Retired Athletes, Brooklyn Busy Executives.

- **G**ive Them a Goal (Purpose — the Dream Outcome)
  - Examples: Pain Free, Celebrity Smile, Never Out Of Breath, Double Your Profit, 7-Figure, 100K.

- **I**ndicate a Time Interval (Timeline)
  - Examples: 4 Hour, 21 Day, 6 Week, 3 Month.
  - ⚠️ Ad platforms restrict quantified claims WITH duration — compliance flag.

- **C**omplete With a Container Word (Method / Vehicle)
  - Examples: Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Incubator, Masterclass, Detox, Experience, Summit, Accelerator, Fast Track, Shortcut, Sprint, Launch, Slingshot, Catapult, Explosion, System, Getaway, Transformation, Mastermind, Game Plan, Deep Dive, Workshop, Comeback, Rebirth, Reset, Hack, Cheatcode, Liftoff.

### Pro tips

- **Rhyme** ("Six-Pack Fast Track") or **Alliterate** ("Make Money Masterclass") — if it flows naturally.
- **Test multiple.** Use the cheapest A/B poll you can run (Instagram story poll, DMs asking "1 or 2?").
- **"Small changes make big differences"** — adding two words ("how to") or removing one ("more") flipped Hormozi's book-title tests by 10-20 points.

## 6. OFFER FATIGUE — rotation order (lightest to heaviest)

Offers fatigue. Local markets fatigue fastest. Rotate in this order, do lightest first:

1. **Change creative** (images / videos)
2. **Change body copy**
3. **Change headline / wrapper**
4. **Change hook wording** (e.g., Lean Challenge → Tone Challenge)
5. **Change seasonal framing**
6. **Change duration**
7. **Change enhancer** (free/discount mechanism)
8. **Change monetization structure** ← $100M Money Models territory; biggest change, last resort

**Rule:** change the wrapper before you change the business.

## 7. FINAL PRE-SHIP CHECKLIST (Mode 3 hand-off to Mode 6)

Before handing off the offer, all of these must be YES:

- [ ] Dream Outcome stated in the buyer's own words, specific and measurable.
- [ ] Value Equation: all four variables scored; weakest named and addressed (Mode 2).
- [ ] Problems exhaustively listed (aim for 20+ before bundling).
- [ ] Bundle solves **every** perceived problem, not most.
- [ ] Core + bonuses stack value eclipses the price.
- [ ] Guarantee uses the "If X by Y, we Z" template — Z is specific.
- [ ] Scarcity type named (1/2/3) — and you can actually sell out.
- [ ] Urgency type named (1/2/3/4) — and the deadline is real.
- [ ] Name uses 3-5 MAGIC components.
- [ ] Price anchored to the cost of inaction (ratio, not absolute).
- [ ] One-sentence repeatability test: buyer can explain it to their partner in one sentence.

**If any are NO, name it and fix before handoff to the client-facing deck (`pitch-deck-architect`).**
